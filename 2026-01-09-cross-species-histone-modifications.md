This study adapts the Sei deep learning architecture to predict histone modifications across plant species. Species-specific models for Arabidopsis, rice, and maize achieve strong within-species accuracy, but transferability declines as phylogenetic distance increases between monocots and dicots. Family-level models — such as a Poaceae model trained jointly on rice and maize — substantially improve generalization to untrained species, demonstrating that phylogenetically informed training serves as an effective inductive bias for plant regulatory genomics. The work also provides an easy-to-use pipeline that generates genome-wide chromatin signal tracks (BedGraph/BigWig) directly from DNA sequence, which is especially valuable for non-model and agriculturally important species.
