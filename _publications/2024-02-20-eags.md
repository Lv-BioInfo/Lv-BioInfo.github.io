EAGS is an imputation method for high-resolution spatial transcriptomics (ST). It builds two kinds of cell patterns — one from gene expression profiles and one from spatial coordinates — derives adaptive smoothing weights from the similarity between cells, and uses those weights to restore gene expression and suppress dropout. On simulated data and real mouse brain and olfactory bulb datasets, EAGS improves clustering accuracy and biological interpretability while substantially reducing runtime and memory compared with existing imputation methods, since it does not rely on a predefined statistical model or iterative GPU training.
